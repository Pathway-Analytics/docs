# Governance

## Overview

Effective governance is essential for ensuring that tariff arrangements operate fairly, transparently, and in accordance with agreed terms across all participating organisations. This document sets out the governance framework for Sexual Health Tariff configurations managed through the Pathway Analytics Tariff Grouper, defining how decisions are made, who holds authority, and how disputes and changes are handled.

## Roles and Responsibilities

### Lead Commissioner

The Lead Commissioner holds primary accountability for the tariff configuration and its ongoing management:

- Owns the tariff configuration and acts as the authoritative point of contact for all participating organisations.
- Approves changes to tariff rates, currency definitions, and configuration parameters
- Communicates updates, decisions, and policy changes to all participating commissioners and providers
- Ensures the configuration reflects current commissioning intentions and local service specifications
- Chairs governance meetings and sets the agenda for periodic reviews

### Participating Commissioners

Participating Commissioners share responsibility for local oversight within the tariff arrangement:

- Maintain local oversight of tariff application within their commissioned services
- Manage relationships with providers operating under the tariff in their area
- Escalate issues, anomalies, or disputes to the Lead Commissioner
- Participate in governance meetings and contribute to decision-making on shared matters
- Ensure local providers comply with data submission requirements

### Providers

Providers are responsible for operational compliance within the tariff framework:

- Submit activity data accurately and within agreed timescales
- Comply with data format specifications and validation requirements
- Implement tariff local codes in accordance with configuration guidance
- Raise queries or concerns through their host commissioner
- Participate in review processes when requested

### Pathway Analytics

Pathway Analytics provides technical administration and advisory support:

- Maintains the Tariff Grouper platform, ensuring system availability and data integrity
- Implements local tariff configurations and changes as directed by the Lead Commissioner
- Provides technical advisory support on tariff structure, currency design, and data requirements
- Produces system reports and data extracts to support governance decision-making
- Escalates technical issues or data quality concerns to the Lead Commissioner

## Change Management

Authority to approve changes to local tariff configuration rests with the Lead Commissioner.

#### Tariff Rate Changes

Tariff rates can be either adjusted globally by a % or factor up or down or they can be adjust individually to new specific rates.  

The request to adjust tariff rates on a local configuration should include the following details:

- The link to the tariff configuration to be changed
- Is this a global adjustment, what is the % move +/-
- Otherwise, The currency to be changed
- The new Primary and Additional tariff rates
- Do these new rates include MFF or any other adjustment, if not what are those adjustments
- The date the change is to be effective from
- If the change is to be retrospective, are prior submissions to be re-processed

#### Modifying Currencies and Triggers

Lead commissioners may adopt local currencies to monitor or charge for specific local activity or cover activity that meets local commissioning requirements.

New currencies or changes to existing currency triggers (the activity definitions that determine tariff groupings) may be requested.  Where currencies track activity that is not covered in the statutory reports (GUMCAD and SRHAD) we may implement local codes to cover this activity, in which case we will require very specific language to describe precisely the activity to be covered.

Change requests to currencies require the following information:

- The link to the tariff configuration to be changed
- The suggested new currency name
- A description of the activity to be included and that which should be excluded, if codes are available, expressing this statement with the use of standard codes will be helpful. Be as precise as possible.
- The new Primary and Additional tariff rates
- Do these new rates include MFF or any other adjustment, if not what are those adjustments
- The date the change is to be effective from
- If the change is to be retrospective, are prior submissions to be re-processed

## Escalation Procedures

### First Line: Pathway Analytics

Pathway Analytics provides the first point of contact for resolving issues:

- Operational queries from providers or participating commissioners are directed to the Lead Commissioner
- The Pathway Analytics aims to resolve matters within 10 working days
- Resolution may involve consultation with Commissioners and providers

### Second Line: Lead Commissioner

Where the Pathway Analytics cannot resolve an issue unilaterally, it is escalated to the Lead Commissioner.

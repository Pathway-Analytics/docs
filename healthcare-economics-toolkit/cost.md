## 4. Costing: three views of one route

Costing is where most local evaluations go wrong, and where care adds most value. The aim is not only to know what was spent, but what each route needed to work and what a commissioner would have to pay to keep it.

### Six steps

1. Identify every resource the route uses.
2. Measure how much of each is used, ideally per person or per contact.
3. Value each resource using a unit cost, stating its source and price year.
4. Allocate shared costs using a stated rule, and test the rule.
5. Separate one-off set-up, evaluation and programme costs from recurrent delivery.
6. Assign each cost to the budget that pays it: council, NHS, funder or other.

### Three cost views, never added together

| View | Question it answers |
|------|---------------------|
| Top-down investment | What the funder bought |
| Bottom-up delivery | What delivery consumed |
| Recurrent continuation | What a commissioner would pay each year |

Other key points in this module: top-down and bottom-up costing as mutual checks; collecting staff time from what people actually do; the retention-driven cost per person-year on PrEP; keeping the PrEP drug out of the council's figures; and where to find UK unit costs (PSSRU/CHE York, NHS National Cost Collection, eMIT).

> **Reading rule:** Hold one master figure per site and cost view; annualise only over active months.
